Overview

In this lesson, we apply percent skills to real-life situations involving discounts, tax, tips, markups, and multi-step price changes. The goal is to decide what to do first, what to do next, and whether the final answer makes sense.

Learning Targets

  • Use percent to solve real-world problems involving money
  • Calculate discounts, tax, tips, and markups accurately
  • Solve multi-step percent problems in the correct order
  • Check whether an answer is reasonable using estimation

Big Idea

In real life, percent is often connected to money and decision making. Many problems are not just one step — they involve a sequence of changes.

Percent problems follow the story. Read carefully, decide the order, and solve one step at a time.

In-Class Work

Today’s focus is applying percent skills in real-life situations. You will work through the Green, Blue, and Black problems using your class handout.

  • Start with Green to build fluency
  • Move to Blue for multi-step problems
  • Challenge yourself with Black problems
  • Use the answer key to check and revise your work

Real-Life Percent Applications

  • Work with discounts, tax, tips, and markups
  • Decide whether the situation shows an increase or decrease
  • Follow the order of the situation one step at a time

Practice & Problem Solving

  • Build fluency with Green questions
  • Solve multi-step Blue problems carefully
  • Use Black questions to compare, reason, and explain

What to Watch For

  • A discount means the price goes down
  • Tax, tip, and markup make the price go up
  • Do not just add or subtract percentages unless it makes sense to do so
  • In multi-step problems, the second percent is often based on a new amount

Green • Blue • Black Tasks

  • Green — Build fluency with one-step percent problems in real contexts
  • Blue — Solve multi-step percent problems involving two changes
  • Black — Compare deals, justify reasoning, and solve deeper percent situations

Show your work clearly so you can check each step and revise mistakes.

Self-Check Reminder

After you finish a question, compare your answer with the key. If your answer does not match, go back and check:

  • Did you use the correct percent?
  • Did you increase or decrease the amount properly?
  • Did you do the steps in the correct order?
  • Does your final answer make sense?
