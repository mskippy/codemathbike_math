Overview

In this lesson, we learn how to calculate percent increase and percent decrease in real-world situations such as markups, discounts, tax, tip, and simple multi-step price changes. The focus is on understanding what happens to the original amount and using efficient multiplier strategies.

Learning Targets

  • Calculate a new value after a percent increase or decrease
  • Use multipliers to represent markups, discounts, tax, and tip
  • Solve one-step and multi-step percent change problems
  • Explain why the order matters when more than one percent change is applied

Big Idea

Percent change is about comparing a new amount to the original amount. A percent increase makes the value larger, while a percent decrease makes the value smaller.

Multipliers help make percent change more efficient:

  • Increase by 20% → multiply by 1.20
  • Decrease by 20% → multiply by 0.80
  • Add 12% tax → multiply by 1.12

The multiplier shows the whole amount plus or minus the percent change.

Lesson Flow

Warm-Up Review

  • Review percent relationships from the previous lessons
  • Find percent of a number and identify the whole, part, and percent
  • Use benchmark strategies and estimation to check answers

Guided Examples

  • Calculate markups and discounts using percent multipliers
  • Find the final cost after tax or tip
  • Work through multi-step situations such as discount then tax

Practice & Problem Solving

  • Solve Green, Blue, and Black questions
  • Compare one-step and multi-step percent change situations
  • Explain how the multiplier matches the situation

Common Percent Change Situations

  • Markup — a store increases the price
  • Discount — a price is reduced
  • Tax — an extra percent is added to the price
  • Tip — an extra percent is added to a bill
  • Interest — money grows by a percent over time

In each case, the key question is: Is the amount increasing or decreasing?

Green • Blue • Black Tasks

  • Green — Practice one-step percent increase and decrease problems
  • Blue — Solve multi-step problems involving discounts, tax, tip, and reverse percent questions
  • Black — Connect percent change with fractions, ratios, and other problem-solving situations

The goal is to notice patterns and become fluent with multipliers, not just follow a rule.

Helpful Reminders

  • Convert the percent to a decimal before multiplying
  • Increase: use a multiplier greater than 1
  • Decrease: use a multiplier less than 1
  • For multi-step problems, do one change at a time
  • Estimate first so you know if your answer makes sense
