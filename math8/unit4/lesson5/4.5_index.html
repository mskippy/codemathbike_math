Overview

In this lesson, we apply percent skills to a real-world shopping task. You will plan food for one full day, compare prices, calculate savings, and find a final total including 12% tax.

Learning Targets

  • Use percent to solve real-world shopping and money problems
  • Calculate sale prices and percent savings accurately
  • Compare name brand and generic items using math
  • Find a final total by adding tax correctly

Big Idea

Percent is useful in real life when comparing prices, spotting deals, and making spending decisions.

Smart shoppers do not just choose items — they compare, calculate, and decide whether a deal is actually worth it.

In-Class Work

Today you will complete the Smart Shopper Challenge using grocery store websites. You may use any of the linked stores to build your meal plan and complete your handout.

  • Plan food for one full day
  • Include breakfast, lunch, dinner, and 2 snacks
  • Stay within the assigned budget
  • Show your calculations clearly on the handout

What You Must Show

  • At least 3 sale calculations
  • At least 3 brand comparisons (name brand vs generic)
  • The percent savings for those comparisons
  • A final total including 12% tax

Your Job

  • Choose realistic food items
  • Record prices carefully
  • Compare options instead of picking the first item you see
  • Make math-based decisions about value

Helpful Reminders

  • Percent off: discount = percent × original price
  • Sale price: original price − discount
  • Percent savings: (difference ÷ original price) × 100
  • Tax: subtotal × 0.12
  • Final total: subtotal + tax

What to Watch For

  • A lower price is not always a better deal unless you compare carefully
  • Be sure you are comparing similar items or sizes
  • Do not forget to include tax at the end
  • Percent savings should be based on the original price
  • Your final choices should be realistic for a full day of food

Success Criteria

  • I included all meals and snacks
  • I showed at least 3 sale calculations
  • I compared 3 brand vs generic items
  • I calculated percent savings correctly
  • I added tax and found a final total
  • My work is clear, organized, and complete
