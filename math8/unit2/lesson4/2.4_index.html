2.4 Ratios Quiz Review

This page is here for students who want to feel confident going into the quiz. If you can follow the structure below calmly and clearly, you are ready.

What the Quiz Is Testing

  • Can you identify what the ratio represents?
  • Can you add total parts correctly?
  • Can you find the value of 1 part?
  • Can you stay organized?
  • Can you answer the actual question being asked?

The quiz is about structure and reasoning — not speed.

Your 5-Step Strategy

  1. Write the ratio clearly.
  2. Add the parts.
  3. Decide what the given number represents:
    • Total?
    • One group?
    • Difference?
  4. Find the value of 1 part.
  5. Multiply and answer the question.

Most mistakes happen in Step 2 or Step 3.

Mini Practice (Green-Level Review)

Try these without notes. Show full work first, then check your answer.

1. Part-to-Whole

The ratio of red to blue balloons is 3 : 8. There are 55 balloons in total.

How many red balloons are there?

Check Answer
Total parts = 3 + 8 = 11
1 part = 55 ÷ 11 = 5
Red balloons = 3 × 5 = 15

2. Part-to-Part (Difference)

The ratio of apples to oranges is 5 : 9. There are 28 more oranges than apples.

How many apples are there?

Check Answer
Difference in parts = 9 − 5 = 4 parts
1 part = 28 ÷ 4 = 7
Apples = 5 × 7 = 35

3. Ratio + Perimeter

The ratio of the length to the width of a rectangle is 4 : 3. The width is 18 cm.

Find the perimeter of the rectangle.

Check Answer
Width = 3 parts = 18 cm → 1 part = 18 ÷ 3 = 6 cm
Length = 4 parts = 4 × 6 = 24 cm
Perimeter = 2(24 + 18) = 2(42) = 84 cm

If you can solve these confidently and neatly, you are in great shape for the quiz.

Final Reminder

  • Write neatly.
  • Label parts clearly.
  • Don’t skip the “value of 1 part” step.
  • Read the last sentence of the question twice.

Calm thinking > rushing.
