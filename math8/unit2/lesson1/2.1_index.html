Overview

In this lesson, we learn how to describe and compare quantities using ratios. The focus is on language, meaning, and interpretation — not formulas or shortcuts.

Learning Targets

  • Understand what a ratio compares
  • Use correct ratio language when speaking and writing
  • Represent ratios using words, numbers, and visuals

Key Vocabulary

  • Ratio — A comparison of two quantities
  • For every — Language used to describe ratios
  • Part-to-part — Comparing one part to another
  • Part-to-whole — Comparing a part to the total

Big Idea

Ratios describe relationships between quantities. They do not tell you how many there are altogether.

The order of a ratio matters — switching the order changes the meaning.

Lesson Flow

Warm-Up

  • Observe visual examples
  • Decide what can be compared
  • Practice saying ratios out loud

Guided Practice

  • Work with ratio cards and visuals
  • Record comparisons in your handout
  • Use numbers, words, and “for every” language

Independent Thinking

  • Represent ratios in multiple ways
  • Explain what a ratio means
  • Check whether a ratio is part-to-part or part-to-whole

Green • Blue • Black Tasks

  • Green — Identify and write basic ratios
  • Blue — Use precise language and classify ratios
  • Black — Create and explain your own ratio examples

Everyone starts with Green. Choose when to move on — challenge is about thinking, not speed.

What You Should Be Recording

  • The quantities being compared
  • Ratios written in more than one way
  • Explanations in your own words
  • Sketches or visual models
