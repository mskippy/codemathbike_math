Integers Review

Today is a practice + review class to help you get ready for your test. You’ll work in groups for two fast activities: Name That Number (Money Edition) and the Solve-the-Problem Relay.

Learning Targets

  • Interpret integers in real contexts (money gained or lost)
  • Use the correct order of operations to simplify expressions
  • Show clear work — one step per line
  • Fix mistakes without skipping steps

Key Idea: Integers as Money

  • Positive (+) = money earned / gained
  • Negative (−) = money spent / lost / owed

Your goal is not speed — it’s accuracy and clear thinking.

Activity 1: Name That Number (Money Edition)

Your group will get the same set of cards as everyone else, but your group will have a different target amount.

The Cards (same for every group)

Card Set: +12, −8, −6, +4, −3, +2

How to Play

  1. Start with 2 cards and make as many expressions as you can that equal the target.
  2. Move to 3 cards, then 4, then 5, then 6.
  3. You may use: + − × ÷ and brackets ( ).
  4. You cannot change the values on the cards.

Homework

Choose a target number from the following sets (choose a number different than the one you used in class)

  • Green: -2, 0, 2, 4
  • Blue: -6, -4, 6, 8
  • Black:-12, -8, 10, 14

What to Record

  • 3 correct expressions that equal your target
  • At least 1 expression with brackets

This is review practice. Focus on good thinking and clear steps.

Activity 2: Solve-the-Problem Relay

This relay is about solving a multi-step expression one line at a time. You must follow the order of operations and keep your work clean.

How the Relay Works

  • Your group receives one expression.
  • Only one person writes at a time.
  • Each person may complete one step only (one operation) on their turn.
  • Pass the paper to the next person after your single step.

Relay Rules (Read Carefully)

  1. One step per turn (one line, one operation).
  2. Follow the order of operations: brackets → multiply/divide left-to-right → add/subtract left-to-right.
  3. Mistakes can be fixed, but fixing a mistake counts as your one step.
  4. No skipping steps (no big mental jumps).
  5. Everyone participates: each group member must write at least one line.

Example (What a Relay Looks Like)

Expression: −3 + 4(2 − 5)²

−3 + 4(−3)²
−3 + 4(9)
−3 + 36
33

When your group finishes, be ready to explain: What was the next “legal” step?

Success Criteria

  • I can explain what a positive or negative integer means in a money situation.
  • I can follow the order of operations without skipping steps.
  • I can write neat, step-by-step work (one step per line).
  • I can spot and fix a mistake without redoing the whole problem.

What’s Next

After these two group activities, we will spend the rest of class doing test review practice. Use your notes and ask questions early if you’re unsure.
